1. FUNDAMENTAL RELATIONSHIPS

The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an object equals
its change in kinetic energy:
W_net = ∫ F_net · ds = ΔK

Where:
- F_net is the net force acting on the object
- ds is the infinitesimal displacement vector
- The integral is taken along the object's path

For a particle of mass m, Newton's second law gives:
F_net = dp/dt = d(mv)/dt

Assuming constant mass (valid for our rubber ball at non-relativistic speeds):
F_net = m dv/dt = m a

Substituting into the work integral:
W_net = ∫ (m dv/dt) · ds

Using the chain rule ds/dt = v, we can rewrite:
W_net = ∫ m v · dv = Δ(½mv²) = ΔK

This confirms the work-energy theorem.

2. MOMENTUM TRANSFER FROM PHOTONS

Each photon carries momentum p_photon = h/λ, where h is Planck's constant
and λ is wavelength.

When a photon interacts with the rubber ball:
- Absorption: Ball gains momentum p_photon
- Reflection: Ball gains up to 2p_photon (for head-on collision)
- Scattering: Ball gains momentum depending on scattering angle

The total momentum transferred to the ball after N photon interactions is:
Δp_ball = Σ_i (momentum transfer per photon i)

In the continuous limit (high photon flux), this becomes:
Δp_ball = ∫ (dp_photon/dt) dt = ∫ F_photon dt

Where F_photon is the average force due to photon momentum transfer.

This integral ∫ F_photon dt equals the total impulse delivered to the ball.

3. WORK DONE BY PHOTON FORCES

The work done by photon forces on the ball as it moves through a displacement

W_photon = ∫ F_photon · ds

Where the integral is taken along the ball's trajectory.
