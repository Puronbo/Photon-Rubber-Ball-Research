SURFACE NETTING MODIFICATIONS
Photon-Sized Rubber Ball Project — Surface Modification Concepts
================================================================

Purpose
-------
This document collects surface modification concepts for the 550 nm photon-sized
rubber ball. "Surface netting" is the running term for controlling the sphere's
interfacial forces (optical, hydration, and quantum) by engineered surface
chemistry and structure. It was referenced by README.md and by other project
documents; restored to match the listing, grounded in the project's own
connections documents.

Why surface effects dominate
----------------------------
At the nanoscale, surface-to-volume ratio is large: for the 550 nm ball, the
hydration shell alone (~1-2 nm thick) is only ~0.4% of the radius, yet interfacial
forces (van der Waals, Casimir-Polder, hydration structuring, adhesion) can
dominate over bulk behaviour. Anything that modifies the surface therefore has an
outsized effect on absorption, adhesion, thermal coupling, and photon momentum
transfer.

Modification channels identified in our documents
-------------------------------------------------
1. Hydrophilic/hydrophobic (wettability) engineering
   - Hydration layers behave as a natural "surface netting" (h2o_and_photon_connections.txt).
   - Nanoscale curvature already alters water structuring; surface chemistry tunes it further.
   - Water's dielectric constant drops from ~78 (bulk) to ~2-10 in the first hydration
     layer because of restricted molecular rotation — a dramatic local field change.
   - Hydrophilic/hydrophobic patterning therefore directly controls hydration
     structure, offering a route to engineer surface forces.

2. Photonic "net" confinement
   - Relates to photon_in_a_net.txt: the sphere's environment acts as a "net"
     whose parameters include refractive index and geometry.
   - Water's refractive index modifies the net parameters; hydration layers can be
     seen as a dynamic, tunable netting around the sphere.
   - Surface modifications affect local field enhancement and force anisotropy
     (photon_engine_research_paper.txt).

3. Quantum-vacuum (Casimir-Polder) tuning
   - Casimir-Polder forces depend on surface geometry and material properties
     (hydrogen_atom_photon_bath.txt), so surface modifications directly tune
     vacuum-fluctuation-mediated attraction.
   - Links to quantum.txt (zero-point energy, confinement) and to the Lamb-shift /
     blackbody-shift thermometry directions in the hydrogen-photon exploration.

4. Mechanical / adhesion coupling
   - Surface energy per area enters JKR adhesion (work of adhesion W): modified
     surfaces change W, hence pull-off force and work of separation in the
     verification model.
   - Enables force isotropy/anisotropy engineering relevant to the photon-engine
     momentum-transfer concept.
