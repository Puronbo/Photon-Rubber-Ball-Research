## 1. FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES: PHOTON MOMENTUM AND FORCES

Photons carry momentum p = h/λ, which can be transferred to objects via:
- **Scattering**: Elastic (Rayleigh/Mie) or inelastic (Raman) processes
- **Absorption**: Followed by re-emission or thermalization
- **Gradient forces**: In non-uniform optical fields (optical tweezers)

For our 550 nm diameter rubber ball (comparable to green photon wavelength):
- Single photon momentum: p = h/λ ≈ (6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s)/(550×10⁻⁹ m) ≈ 1.2×10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s
- Radiation pressure force for 1 mW laser focused to 1 μm²: ~6.6 pN
- This is comparable to forces measured in atomic force microscopy and
  sufficient to manipulate nanoscale objects

## 2. OPTICAL FORCES ON PHOTON-SIZED OBJECTS

### 2.1 Scattering and Gradient Forces
In optical tweezers, particles experience:
- **Scattering force**: Along beam direction, proportional to intensity
- **Gradient force**: Toward intensity maximum, proportional to ∇I

For particles comparable to wavelength (Mie regime):
- Complex interference patterns affect force distribution
- Both transverse and axial trapping possible
- Force magnitude depends on refractive index contrast

**Relevance to our rubber ball**:
- Refractive index of rubber (~1.5) vs. medium (air/water) provides contrast
- Size parameter x = 2πr/λ ≈ 1 for 550 nm ball in 550 nm light → Mie regime
- Optical forces can exceed Brownian forces for sufficient laser power

### 2.2 Optical Potential Wells
Intensity gradients create conservative potentials:
U(r) = - (α/2) |E(r)|²
where α is polarizability

For our photon-sized ball:
- Polarizability enhanced near Mie resonances
- Potential well depth can be tuned via laser wavelength and power
- Multiple stable positions possible due to scattering resonances

### 3.2 Quantum Recoil and Heisenberg Limit
Each photon scattering event imparts recoil:
Δv = p_photon / m_ball

- Mass ≈ 8.7×10⁻¹⁷ kg

- To reach 1 m/s requires ~7×10¹⁰ photons
