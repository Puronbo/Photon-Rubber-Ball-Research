## Abstract

This proposal outlines a comprehensive research program to explore connections between a photon-sized rubber ball verification system and fundamental physics theories, with particular focus on quantum gravity phenomenology, black hole physics, quantum thermodynamics, and quantum foundations. Building on extensive theoretical work demonstrating how this table-top experimental platform can access regimes relevant to Planck-scale physics, black hole thermodynamics, and quantum measurement theory, we propose a coordinated experimental and theoretical effort to develop the verification system into a precision probe of fundamental physics. The program integrates theoretical development, experimental implementation, and data analysis to test specific predictions from quantum gravity models, decoherence theories, and entropic gravity hypotheses using controlled photon impacts on dielectric microspheres.

## 1. Introduction and Motivation

The unification of quantum mechanics and general relativity remains the foremost challenge in fundamental physics. While direct probing of the Planck scale ($E_P \approx 10^{19}$ GeV) is currently infeasible, table-top experiments offer indirect pathways through analogies, sensitivity to weak effects, and symmetry principles. Recent theoretical advances suggest that microsphere optomechanical systems subjected to controlled photon impacts can serve as probes of quantum gravity effects, black hole analogs, and quantum measurement phenomena.

Our prior work has established that a photon-sized rubber ball verification system (275nm diameter, 1100 kg/m³ density, 50 MPa Young's modulus) verifies nine distinct physics axes and can be theoretically extended to connect to:
- Quantum thermodynamics (fluctuation theorems from photon impact work distributions)
- Decoherence theory (competition between environmental and gravitational decoherence)
- Entropic gravity (Verlinde's hypothesis applied to JKR adhesion energy)
- Black hole physics (scattering analogs, thermodynamic quantities, horizon analogs)
- Fluctuation-dissipation theory (viscoelastic models and quantum dissipation)
- Cognitive theories (Orch-OR, IIT, participatory universe, QBism)

This proposal seeks to transform these theoretical connections into an experimental reality through a phased development program.

## 3. Background and Significance

### 3.1 Theoretical Foundation
Our theoretical work has established multiple, independent connections between the verification system and frontier physics:

**Quantum Thermodynamics Connection:** Photon impacts constitute work impulses on the sphere's mechanical degrees of freedom. Sequences of impacts generate work distributions that should satisfy fluctuation theorems (Jarzynski, Crooks) if the measurement process is thermodynamically consistent. Deviations could signal breakdown of classical descriptions or novel physics.

**Decoherence Connection:** The sphere experiences decoherence from photon scattering (environmental, tunable) and potentially from gravitational effects (Penrose-Diosi models). By tuning photon flux, we can explore the crossover between decoherence regimes and test whether gravitational decoherence becomes detectable above environmental noise.

**Entropic Gravity Connection:** The JKR adhesion energy (Axis 6) may have an entropic origin analogous to Verlinde's hypothesis, where gravity emerges from entropy gradients associated with information positions. Precision force measurements can test whether adhesion forces follow entropic force predictions.

**Black Hole Connection:** Mie scattering of photons by the sphere preserves information (unitary S-matrix), analogous to unitarity questions in black hole evaporation. Additionally, relativistic impacts connect to Unruh and Hawking temperature analogs, while the hoop conjecture provides a threshold for gravitational collapse analogs.

**Fluctuation-Dissipation Connection:** The viscoelastic restitution model (Axis 3) connects to fluctuation-dissipation theorems and quantum dissipation models (Caldeira-Leggett), providing links to dissipation in quantum gravity and cosmological contexts.

### 3.2 Experimental Feasibility
Key experimental capabilities are accessible with current technology:
- Optical tweezers and trapped microspheres (standard in biophysics and colloid science)
- Precision force measurement (AFM, optical trapping calibration)
- Scattering measurements (standard light scattering techniques)
- Environmental control (vacuum, temperature, vibration isolation)
- Single-photon detection and counting
- Feedback control and quantum state engineering

### 3.3 Significance
Success in this program would:
- Establish a table-top platform for quantum gravity phenomenology
- Provide experimental tests of black hole information paradox analogs
- Probe the quantum-to-classical transition in a controlled system
- Test fundamental assumptions in quantum measurement theory

- Develop techniques for precision force and work measurements at the microscale
- Create a versatile platform for interdisciplinary physics exploration

## 5. Methodology

### 5.1 Core Experimental Platform
The experimental platform will consist of:
- **Optical Trapping System:** 1064nm trapping laser, high NA objective, piezoelectric stage for 3D positioning
- **Photon Delivery System:** Tunable laser (400-1100nm) with AOM for pulse shaping and flux control, single-photon attenuation capabilities
- **Position Detection System:** Homodyne interferometry or quadrant photodiode with signal processing for nm/√Hz sensitivity
- **Force Detection System:** AFM cantilever or optical trap displacement calibration for pN-√N force sensitivity
- **Scattering Detection System:** Multi-angle photodiode array or imaging system for scattering pattern measurement
- **Environmental Control:** Vacuum chamber (10⁻⁶ to 10⁻³ mbar), temperature stabilization (4-400K), vibration isolation (stage and acoustic)
- **Control and Data Acquisition:** Real-time processor (FPGA or RT Linux) with feedback loops, high-speed data storage

### 5.2 Measurement Protocols
**Quantum Thermodynamics Protocol:**
1. Trap single sphere in vacuum at controlled temperature
2. Apply sequence of photon impacts with controlled timing, flux, and polarization
3. Track sphere trajectory with high temporal resolution
4. Calculate work for each trajectory segment: W = ΔKE + ΔPE + W_diss
5. Build work distribution from ensemble of trajectories (N ≥ 10,000)
6. Test fluctuation theorems: compute ⟨e^(-βW)⟩ and P_F(W)/P_R(-W)

**Decoherence Competition Protocol:**
1. Trap sphere in controlled environment (vacuum, temperature)
2. Apply photon flux Φ and measure decoherence rate via loss of motional coherence
3. Vary Φ over 6-8 orders of magnitude (10¹⁰ to 10²⁴ photons/m²/s)
4. Measure coherence time or heating rate as function of Φ
5. Subtract known environmental contributions (gas collisions, blackbody radiation)
6. Compare residual decoherence rate to gravitational model predictions

**Entropic Gravity Protocol:**
1. Approach sphere toward surface using optical trap or AFM cantilever
2. Measure force-distance curve with pN resolution
3. Measure work of separation via integration of force-distance curve
4. Vary temperature and measure temperature dependence of adhesion
5. Compare measured work and force to entropic predictions: W = TΔS, F = T dS/dx
6. Test for linear temperature dependence predicted by entropic models

**Black Hole Analog Protocol:**
1. Trap sphere and apply controlled photon flux
2. Measure angle-resolved scattering pattern and polarization
3. Extract scattering cross-section and compare to Mie theory calculations
4. Compare to geometric cross-section (πR²) and Rayleigh limit (∝ 1/λ⁴)
5. Measure scattering analogies to black hole greybody factors (frequency-dependent absorption)
6. For relativistic impacts: use higher energy photons or particle beams to access v > 0.001c regime
7. Measure energy and momentum spectra of scattered particles/radiation
8. Test for Unruh and Hawking temperature analogs in radiation spectra

### 5.3 Theoretical Analysis and Modeling
Complementary theoretical work will include:
- Numerical simulation of sphere dynamics under photon impacts (Langevin equation with measurement back-action)
- Calculation of expected work distributions for various impact models
- Modeling of decoherence channels and their relative contributions
- Calculation of expected adhesion forces from entropic and energetic models
- Prediction of scattering cross-sections and analogs to black hole quantities
- Development of unified framework connecting all measurements
- Bayesian model comparison to evaluate competing hypotheses
