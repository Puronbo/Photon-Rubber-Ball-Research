## 5. Mathematical Formulation

### 5.1 Light as Electromagnetic Derivative
In classical electrodynamics:
$$\vec{E} = -\nabla\phi - \frac{\partial\vec{A}}{\partial t}, \quad \vec{B} = \nabla\times\vec{A}$$
The photon as quantum of this field represents quantized derivatives of the potentials.

In quantum field theory:
$$\hat{A}^\mu(x) = \int \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2\omega_p}} \left( \hat{a}_p^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat{a}_p^{\mu\dagger} e^{ip\cdot x} \right)$$

### 5.2 Instantaneous Thermalization Model
For photon impact on matter:
$$\frac{dQ}{dt} = \sigma \Phi \left(1 - R\right) \left(1 - e^{-\alpha d}\right) \cdot \mathcal{T}(t)$$
where:
- $\sigma$: Stefan-Boltzmann constant (for thermal radiation context)
- $\Phi$: photon flux
- $R$: reflectance
- $\alpha$: absorption coefficient
- $d$: material thickness
- $\mathcal{T}(t)$: thermalization response function (often modeled as exponential)

In the instantaneous limit ($\mathcal{T}(t) \rightarrow \delta(t)$):
$$Q = \sigma \Phi \left(1 - R\right) \left(1 - e^{-\alpha d}\right) \cdot t_{impact}$$

### 5.3 Derivative-Based Action Principle
The electromagnetic action:
$$S_{EM} = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0} \int F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu} d^4x$$
where $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is the derivative of the potential.
Photons are quanta of this derivative-based theory.
